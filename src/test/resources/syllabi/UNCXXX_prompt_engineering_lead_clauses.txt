 

UNC***: PROMPT ENGINEERING 
L T P Cr 
2 0 2 3.0 
 
Course Objective: This course aims to provide students with a structured and practical understanding 
of Prompt Engineering, which is the systematic design of inputs (prompts) for Large Language 
Models (LLMs) to obtain reliable, accurate, and controllable outputs. The course focuses on prompt 
patterns, optimization strategies, evaluation techniques, safety aspects, and responsible use of 
Generative AI tools in engineering and academic contexts. 
 
Syllabus: 
Introduction to Prompt Engineering; Basic idea of Generative AI tools; Understanding prompts and 
their role in AI interaction; Key components of a good prompt; General limitations of AI responses; 
Responsible use of AI in academics 
 
Basic Prompting Techniques; Writing clear and effective instructions; Using simple examples in 
prompts; Role-based prompting for different tasks; Creating reusable prompt formats; Improving 
responses through prompt refinement 
 
Prompting for Complex Tasks; Breaking tasks into smaller steps; Asking for structured and stepwise 
outputs; Using prompts for extended tasks and workflows; Applying constraints for better control; 
Prompting in common academic and engineering scenarios 
 
Evaluation and Ethical Use; Checking prompt outputs for correctness and relevance; Basic 
comparison of different prompts; Reducing incorrect or misleading responses; Safe prompting 
practices; Ethical concerns such as privacy and academic integrity 
 
Familiarization with standard: IEEE 610.12-1990 (Standard Glossary of Software Engineering 
Terminology), IEEE 7000-2021 (Model Process for Addressing Ethical Concerns during System 
Design), IEEE 7001-2021 (Transparency of Autonomous Systems), IEEE 7002-2022 (Data Privacy 
Process), IEEE 1012-2016 (System and Software Verification and Validation), IEEE 730-2014 
(Software Quality Assurance Processes), and IEEE 1061-1998 (Software Quality Metrics). 
 
Laboratory Work: The laboratory component of this course provides hands-on practice in designing 
and improving prompts for Generative AI systems. Students will learn basic prompt writing, use of 
examples, role-based prompting, structured response generation, and stepwise prompting for larger 
tasks. The labs also introduce simple evaluation methods and responsible use of AI tools in 
engineering contexts. 
 
Course learning outcome (CLO):  The student will be able to: 
1. Understand the basic concept of prompt engineering and its role in Generative AI systems. 
2. Write clear and effective prompts using common prompting techniques. 
3. Apply prompt design methods for structured and multi-step engineering tasks. 
4. Evaluate AI-generated responses and demonstrate responsible and ethical use of Generative 
AI tools.
